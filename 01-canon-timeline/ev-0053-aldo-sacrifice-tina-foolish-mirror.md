## Summary

Rather than attempt the kill-task alone — which he recognizes as effectively suicidal — Aldo turns himself in to the Federation, handing his rank and title over to Tina on his way out and framing it, with visible effort, as an opportunity rather than a loss. Tina is left to break the news to Foolish, who initially assumes something trivial (did Aldo get fired again?) before the truth lands in stages: not fired, taken; and worse, once Foolish realizes their earlier plan (Foolish personally killing Regime members) had been the Federation's actual objection — it had to be Aldo doing the killing specifically, not him, or the arrangement doesn't count. Foolish's relief that "we're gonna get Juan back" curdles almost immediately into guilt once he understands what buying that back cost.

What follows is a quiet, deliberate mirroring: Foolish calls an emergency conference for an audience of two, the way Juan always would have; Tina starts repeating Aldo's own phrasing back at people. Two immortals — one part-totem, one part-demon — stepping into the roles their absent mortal counterparts used to hold, missing them more than either says outright. Separately, Foolish tries the same performance with Quackity (freshly escaped his own imprisonment, with help from a fellow prisoner named Pepino), insisting on the "plus plus" title and rattling off a list of chores in a register that's pure Juan. Alondrissa later names the pattern outright to both of them — "you are like Juan and you are like Aldo" — landing very differently for each: delight from Tina, something closer to offended disbelief from Foolish. ## Consequences

- Establishes Tina as acting Generalísima and Foolish as effectively running the North's day-to-day in Juan's absence — a real structural shift, not just an emotional one.
- The Foolish/Tina mirroring of Juan/Aldo is a strong, sustained image worth carrying forward: two immortals covering for two absent mortals, performing competence while visibly grieving underneath it.
