## Summary

Juan is released without warning, unsure at first whether it's a trap, and makes his way home hurt and disoriented — checking Foolish's dragon first, finding no one there, before reaching the North castle. Graf and Tina's reactions (visible shock at his physical state, a well-meaning but deeply misjudged gift of a Cucurucho-faced stuffed animal that sends Juan into a panic) establish just how rough the return is before he's even had a chance to process it. Small, almost absurd domestic anxieties surface immediately and involuntarily — missing Purple Wednesday, the state of the house, whether Vegetta (also unaccounted for, but just asleep) is safe — a pattern consistent with how Juan has always coped under stress by fixating on order and upkeep. The real blow lands once Tina tells him Aldo never came back: Juan doesn't believe her at first, searches Aldo's room and the meeting room himself, and only accepts it once he's confirmed the emptiness firsthand. A separate, quieter conversation with Alondra later has Juan recount the captivity again, this time breaking down mid-sentence trying to explain the uncertainty of whether Aldo — or he himself, if the Federation doesn't honor its word — will ever really be free of this. [CANON FACT]

That night, alone in his office, Juan looks at a photo Foolish had left him and finds Foolish's goodbye note, laughing despite everything at the note's final joke.

## Consequences

- Establishes that Juan's release and Aldo's continued captivity aren't linked the way anyone assumed — Aldo turning himself in didn't actually secure Juan's freedom on any predictable schedule, undermining any sense that the Federation is operating on terms the North can trust or plan around.
- Juan's compulsive fixation on house upkeep and missed rituals (Purple Wednesday, cleaning) immediately upon return is worth tracking as a returning/escalating trait — matches earlier-established notes about his OCD-adjacent tendencies worsening under stress.
