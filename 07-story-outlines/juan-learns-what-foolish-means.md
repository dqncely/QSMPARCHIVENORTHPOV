## Premise

Juan has been quietly working on his English pronunciation and vocabulary, a few minutes a day in his office, since it isn't his first language. Foolish finds him at it and offers — half-teasing, half-genuine — that they could just talk to each other instead of Juan studying alone like he's cramming for a test. Foolish could use the help with his own Spanish too. The two end up trading language lessons badly: Foolish is the more careful one, testing conjugations out loud (duerme.. duermir.. dormir?) trying to get it exactly right; Juan doesn't worry about precision and finds Foolish's fumbled Spanish far more irritating than Foolish finds his English — but they end up laughing at each other more than actually correcting each other. Scene ends with Juan finding out that "Foolish" translates roughly to "necio" — and being delighted by it.
