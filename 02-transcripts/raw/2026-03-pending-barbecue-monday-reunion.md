## Notes

Same mixed-source convention as prior entries. Per author: this is the
North's regular outdoor cookout/karaoke setup on the mansion roof —
grills, seating, a TV and mics, built up over months mostly by Juan; one
of the things that sets The North apart from other factions is how lived-in
it feels, everyone having contributed something. Also per author: Juan
and Foolish have a running tradition of trying to match each other's
drinking pace, competitively, revived tonight for the first time since
Juan's return.

## Content — karaoke (song lyrics omitted; scene preserved)

As the sun sets, Juan offers the group a choice — musical chairs or
karaoke; Mike leans toward karaoke and Juan lets the guests decide.
Foolish and Mike dance around in the background while Juan, already
louder than usual, defers to Tina first — she deflects, suggesting Juan
should record a love song "for Aldo," which he laughs off and agrees to
do "later."

Juan goes first, as he usually does, to warm the group up. He picks a
**Spanish-language reggaeton love song** (title not specified in the
source) about missing someone intensely and wanting them back — not as
friends. He fumbles the opening lines, catches the rhythm once it slows,
and performs it with real feeling despite slurring some lines. Foolish
and Mike cheer him on. Tina, catching a few words about "coming back,"
asks if it's about Aldo; Juan clarifies it's just "about love," and
firmly denies (twice) that it's for Aldo or anyone specific when pressed
again later in the song. Foolish, listening, admits to Tina he has no
idea what the song is about but is enjoying it anyway — Juan has always
had that effect, making his meaning land even for people who don't
speak Spanish.

Pierre arrives partway through, greeted warmly. Mike goes next with a
**Spanish-language song** (title: *Eres*) about love, badly mangled
lyrically but sung with enthusiasm; everyone joins in for the chorus
despite two English speakers and one French speaker butchering the
pronunciation.

Tina is next, initially says she doesn't have a song ready; the group
suggests Pierre sing something in French instead. Pierre performs a
**comedic French song** with crude, deliberately silly content — the
gist, once translated at the group's insistent request, involves
someone urinating everywhere and the domestic chaos that causes; the
translation lands as intended, and everyone (Juan especially) finds it
hilarious.

Foolish goes next with **"Can You Feel the Love Tonight"** (Elton
John/The Lion King) — he doesn't really "sing" during karaoke nights, per
the author, preferring exaggerated funny voices that reliably make a
certain someone laugh; tonight is no exception, with Juan giggling
through the whole performance, especially a bit toward the end where
Foolish's voice starts sounding like Toad from Mario.

Finally Tina, now warmed up by everyone else's turns, asks if the group
knows **Hannah Montana**; Juan only knows the theme song ("Best of Both
Worlds" — title, not lyrics, given here) from watching Power Rangers-era
TV as a kid, badly mangling what he remembers of it with Foolish's help.
For her actual turn, Tina picks **"If We Were a Movie"** (Hannah
Montana); Foolish claims to know it (he doesn't); partway through, sensing
Tina losing steam, Foolish and then Juan join in on the chorus to carry
her through it.
