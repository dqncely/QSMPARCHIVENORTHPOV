## Author's analysis (paraphrased/summarized, not verbatim beyond the quoted lines below)

Juan's love, in this scene, reads as dramatic, loyal past the point of
reason, self-mythologizing, and humiliation-resistant — until it suddenly
collapses into rage. He gives Cucurucho a hard ultimatum, then
immediately starts softening it, extending it, explaining it to Teddy,
reframing the waiting itself as proof that he tried "hasta el último
momento." He can set a boundary verbally, but emotionally keeps
reopening the door.

The clearest line, per the author, is his thesis statement:

> **Juan:** "Porque cuando yo amo, amo de verdad, cabrón."

For Juan, love isn't casual or "let's see where this goes" — it's
totalizing. If he loves, he commits completely, even when the other
person hasn't earned it. This is offered by the author as the reason a
later situation with **Graf** (a North member; sincere but unreciprocated
romantic interest in Juan) becomes a real crisis for Juan — he
understands too well what intense love means, and doesn't want to accept
it from someone without returning it in kind, which he'd consider
dishonest.

There's also a strong insecurity thread: Juan asks Teddy if he's ever
felt like he loves more than he's loved back, then says that's basically
been true his whole life. This is offered as context for his later
possessiveness/jealousy — not pure ego, but a fear of always being the
one giving more. Exclusivity reads to him as proof of being enough; if
someone else has other loyalties or "special friends," his instinct reads
that as "then I am not enough."

## Directly quoted monologue

> **Juan:** "No. No voy a hacer esto otra vez. No voy a sentarme como pendejo esperando que alguien se dé cuenta. No voy a escribir ultimátums en mi cabeza. No voy a mirar la puerta cada cinco minutos como si fuera a entrar con flores. Ya aprendí. Ya me rompieron el corazón de la manera más estúpida posible."
