## Content — wardrobe context (author summary, not a scene)

Per author: Juan has seen Foolish in a range of revealing outfits before
this — a tight purple cropped shirt and short purple shorts for a recent
Purple Wednesday court hearing with Katie and Erwoon, 90s fitness
instructor gear (red shorts, tank top) for PvP training the day before.
Oddly, those outfits read as more revealing than Foolish's default look
(no shirt, baggy utility pants/sweats, heavy gold jewelry at the waist,
neck, and arms) — Juan's used to the default; the costume outfits are
what actually catch him off guard.

Juan's own style has shifted over time: shirtless swim trunks on
arrival (vacation mode), then formal button-downs and tucked-in pants
once he started working under Vegetta — business casual, novelty ties,
form-fitting. Plus the various coordinated joke outfits with Foolish.
He's never worn anything as revealing as what's about to happen tonight.

## Content — the morning after (author summary)

The next morning, Foolish goes to Juan's office mostly to bother him
after a long stretch working on the temple — and, per the author,
partly hoping for another look at last night's outfit. Instead, Juan's
wearing his Purple Wednesday costume (the tight purple crop and shorts
mentioned above). Foolish realizes he's been the only one to see Juan in
either look — Juan never even bothered to wake Tina, the one responsible
for the altered clothes, that night. He recalls how Juan had turned to
face away from him unprompted, and the tattoo. He decides not to think
about it further.

## Author's note — where things stand

Both are now "semi-aware" of their own feelings. Juan is testing the
water, hinting without asking outright, without much success — Foolish
either plays along casually or freaks out internally without showing it.
Foolish is actively trying to avoid crossing an emotional threshold;
Juan has less concern about that specifically, but more concern about
crossing lines in other ways, given he's technically Foolish's superior
and that superior (Vegetta) has his own history with Foolish. There's
also a contrast in how alcohol affects each of them: Juan becomes bolder
about saying/doing risky things when drunk, while Foolish becomes more
withdrawn/careful in the same state. Physically, per the author: Foolish
gets more touchy when affected, Juan gets more "feely" (emotionally
open) rather than physical.
